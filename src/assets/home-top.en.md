One Among Us has been established for nearly five years now. Five years pass in the snap of a finger, and the site has already published the stories of more than a hundred departed souls.

One person on earth, one star in the sky. When a person on earth passes away, a star in the sky slips away. Amid the vast sea of stars, this is only a tiny, tiny patch of night sky—so small it could not be smaller. Sometimes I wonder: how long must I walk on these two feet, how long must I search, before I can trace out an entire star map?

Those stars in the night sky do not need our praise—even if no one records them, they will never be forgotten. Just as there is no need to ask which blooming flower was nourished by the spring soil, there is no need to ask which child afraid of the dark once borrowed that flickering, firefly-like starlight, shouldered their pack, and set out on the road away from home.

But the pen in my hand that traces the star map can never stop—not because I want to remember, but because I want to forget. Because I want the relief of "shaking myself free with one shrug." Because I want to no longer miss and "need" you, who have already departed. Because I want to ask you one last gatha, and then say a forever goodbye.

So I must thank every one of our readers. By comparison, as the writer, "I" may in fact be the most selfish person of all. After all, only when "we" forever remember these stories that allow us to recognize one another, only then can every "I" within them forget just a little.
